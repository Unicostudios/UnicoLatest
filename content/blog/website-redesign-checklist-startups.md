A website redesign works when you do the steps in order: audit what exists, set one goal, fix the content and structure, design, build, protect your SEO with redirects, then test and launch. Most failed redesigns skip the audit or the redirect plan. The checklist below is the order we use with founders in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Dubai and Singapore.

## When does a startup actually need a redesign?

You need a redesign when the site no longer does its job: visitors can't tell what you do in a few seconds, it doesn't match your brand or product, or nobody on the team can edit it without a developer. If the message is right and pages are only slow or dated, targeted fixes are cheaper.

Signs a redesign is justified:

- You've changed what you sell, who you sell to or how you're priced.
- You're raising money and the site undersells the company.
- The logo, website and product look like different companies (see [why that happens and how to fix it](/blog/brand-website-app-consistency)).
- The site is hard to update, so it's always out of date.
- Mobile visitors struggle to use it.

## What do you do before design starts?

Before design starts, record where the site stands today and decide what it must achieve. Without a baseline you can't tell afterwards whether the redesign worked, and without a goal every opinion in the room carries equal weight.

**Phase 1: Audit and goals**

1. Write one primary goal: enquiries, demo bookings, sign-ups or purchases. One goal, not five.
2. Pull the last 6 to 12 months of analytics: top pages, traffic sources, conversion rate, mobile share.
3. Export your current URLs (from the sitemap or a crawl) and mark which get traffic or have inbound links.
4. Note the queries you already appear for in Google Search Console.
5. List what visitors ask in sales calls and on WhatsApp. These become page content.
6. Name one decision-maker. Design by committee is the top cause of slipped timelines.

## How should you plan content and structure?

Plan the pages and write the key copy before designing them. Design built around placeholder text produces layouts that break when real words arrive, and it hides the real question: what are we trying to say?

**Phase 2: Content and structure**

7. Draw a page map. For most startups: Home, What we do (one page per service or product), Work or Customers, About, Contact, and a small number of guides.
8. Write the homepage message first: who it's for, what you do, why you, what to do next. Test it on someone outside the company.
9. Decide which current pages to keep, merge or retire. Don't delete a page that ranks or has backlinks; redirect it.
10. Collect proof you can actually show: named customers (with permission), real results, real screenshots. Don't publish claims you can't back.
11. Write calls to action page by page. Each page gets one main next step.

## What belongs in the design phase?

The design phase should produce a system, not just screens: colours, type, spacing and components that the site and your product share. Starting from your brand rules also means the site can be built faster and stays consistent when you add pages later.

**Phase 3: Design**

12. Confirm brand basics: logo, colours, typefaces, tone of voice. If these are weak or missing, fix them first. This is where a [brand identity and website done by one team](/services) saves rework.
13. Design mobile first. For most Indian audiences the phone is the main screen.
14. Design the real states: forms with errors, empty results, long names, slow connections.
15. Check contrast and tap targets for accessibility.
16. Get sign-off on the homepage and one inner page before designing the rest.

## What should you check during the build?

The build is where speed, search visibility and editability are won or lost. Choose a stack your team can edit, and make performance and metadata part of the build rather than a clean-up afterwards. We build in-house with Next.js for this reason; see [our web and app development](/bengaluru-web-app-development).

**Phase 4: Build**

17. Every page has a unique title (about 60 characters), a meta description (about 155), one H1 and a canonical URL.
18. Images are compressed and sized; the largest image on each page loads fast.
19. Forms deliver to a real inbox or CRM and you've tested each one yourself.
20. Analytics and conversion tracking are installed and verified.

## How do you redesign without losing SEO?

You protect SEO by mapping every old URL to a new one before launch and adding a 301 redirect for each URL that changes. Search engines and AI answer engines both rely on stable URLs; broken links throw away years of accumulated trust.

**Phase 5: SEO safeguards**

21. Build a redirect sheet: old URL, new URL, status. No old URL left without a destination.
22. Keep titles and headings on pages that already rank unless you have a reason to change them.
23. Update `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt`, and confirm the staging site is not indexable but production is.

## What do you test at launch, and after?

Test the live site yourself, then watch it for a few weeks. Launch day is the start of measurement, not the end of the project.

**Phase 6: Launch and follow-up**

24. Pre-launch: click every link, submit every form, test on a real phone, run the redirect sheet, check pages in a private browser window.
25. After launch: submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console, watch for crawl errors and drops in the first weeks, and compare against the baseline from step 2.

### Quick reference: redesign or repair?

| Situation | Better move |
|---|---|
| Message is clear, pages are slow | Repair: performance fixes |
| Brand has changed or is inconsistent | Redesign with the brand system |
| Can't edit without a developer | Rebuild on an editable stack |
| Ranking pages, weak conversion | Keep URLs; redesign layout and copy |
| Pre-fundraise, site undersells you | Redesign homepage and proof first |
